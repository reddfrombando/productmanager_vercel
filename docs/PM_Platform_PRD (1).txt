1. Overview
This platform is a self-paced, free-resource-driven curriculum for learning product management end to end — from foundational concepts through AI product management, packaged as a structured, phase-based learning journey with embedded video tutorials, progress tracking, self-assessment, and a growing library of learner-built portfolio projects and case studies.
It is built on top of a curated content taxonomy (12 phases, 24 modules, 180–220 topics) that already exists as a compiled resource list, and is designed to be continuously enriched over time by input from practicing product managers via a structured contributor pipeline.
1.1 Problem Statement
People trying to break into or grow in product management face two problems today:
	•	Free, high-quality PM content is scattered across YouTube channels, newsletters, blogs, and paid courses, with no single structured path connecting them.
	•	Most free content stops at “what is a PM” and rarely progresses to applied, portfolio-grade practice (PRDs, case studies, mock interviews) that actually gets someone hired.
This platform's job is to organize existing free knowledge into a guided curriculum and pair it with practical, assessable exercises — without requiring the learner to pay for it.
1.2 Goals
	•	Give a self-directed learner a clear, phase-by-phase path through product management, from fundamentals to AI PM.
	•	Make progress visible and motivating (tracking, completion state, light assessment) without turning the product into a heavyweight LMS.
	•	Build a continuously improving content pipeline sourced from real, currently-practicing PMs, curated by an admin before publishing.
	•	Keep the experience simple, visual, and fast to navigate — not a wall of text or a spreadsheet-like table.
1.3 Non-Goals (for v1)
	•	Not building proprietary video content — v1 embeds existing YouTube/Vimeo tutorials, not original filming.
	•	Not a certification or accreditation product.
	•	Not a cohort-based or live-instructor product — fully self-paced.
	•	Not a general LMS for arbitrary subjects — scoped specifically to product management.

3. Information Architecture
Content is organized in a strict three-level hierarchy, matching the existing curriculum compilation:
	•	Phase (e.g., “Phase 1 — Product Thinking Foundations”) — top-level stage of the journey
	•	Module (e.g., “Module 2 — Customer Discovery”) — a themed unit within a phase
	•	Topic (e.g., “Jobs To Be Done”) — an individual concept with its own tutorial links, notes area, and assessment
The current source curriculum (from the compiled brochure) spans 12 phases, 24 modules, and 180–220 topics, plus 12 portfolio projects and 40+ case studies. This taxonomy is the v1 content backbone; see Appendix A for the full phase/module list.
4. Core User Flows & Features
4.1 Public Landing Page
Purpose: explain the platform and convert visitors into signed-up learners.
	•	Hero section introducing the platform as a free, compiled, structured path through product management.
	•	Scrollable overview of the phase structure (visual, not a plain table) — gives a preview of the journey without requiring sign-in.
	•	Primary CTA: “Sign in / Get started”.
	•	Secondary, visually distinct entry point (bottom-left): “Are you a Product Manager? Help us build this.” — routes to the contributor flow (see 4.5).
4.2 Authentication
	•	Simple sign-in/sign-up (email or SSO — provider TBD, see Open Questions).
	•	No paywall or approval gate for learners — anyone can sign in and start.
4.3 Learning Journey Navigation
	•	Post-login home: visual, card-based view of all Phases (not a dense table). Each phase card is clickable.
	•	Phase view: shows that phase's Modules as an interactive grid (suggested: ~6 tiles per view, expandable), not a plain list.
	•	Module view: clicking a module tile expands/enlarges into a detail view showing that module's Topics, with a clear back-navigation to the previous screen at every level.
	•	Topic view: embedded tutorial video(s) (YouTube/Vimeo or other), a personal notes panel next to or below the video, and a completion control.
4.4 Progress Tracking & Assessment
	•	Per-topic completion state, settable manually and (where feasible) inferred automatically from video watch progress.
	•	A persistent progress panel/tab showing overall completion and which modules/topics remain.
	•	Each module includes a lightweight assessment or applied exercise (e.g., a short problem, prompt, or mini-project) so the learner demonstrates practical understanding, not just video-watching — maps to the portfolio projects and case studies in the source curriculum (see Appendix B).
	•	Notes taken while watching are saved and revisitable per topic.
4.5 Contributor Pipeline (“Are you a PM?”)
A separate, lightweight flow for sourcing new content from practicing product managers, decoupled from the learner experience.
	•	Entry point on the landing page routes contributors to a short external-shareable form (e.g., embedded form or a distinct link sent directly to PMs).
	•	Form asks, per topic/module: “Any tutorial you'd recommend for this topic?” and “Any topic you have extra expertise in, plus a resource (link, book, PDF) for it?”
	•	Submissions land in a review queue (not published automatically) — see Admin Panel.
4.6 Admin Panel
Admin-only area (not visible to learners) for curating and publishing content.
	•	Content database view: all phases/modules/topics and their current published resources.
	•	Submission queue: incoming contributor suggestions, with Approve / Reject / Edit-before-publish actions.
	•	Publishing status per topic: Draft, Pending Approval, Published.
	•	Basic reporting: how much of the curriculum has published content vs. gaps still needing resources.
5. Non-Functional Requirements
	•	Performance: phase/module navigation should feel instant (client-side transitions, no full page reloads between levels).
	•	Usability: the browsing experience should feel visual and exploratory (card/tile based), explicitly not a dense spreadsheet-style table — this was a specific product requirement from the source brief.
	•	Responsiveness: full functionality on both desktop and mobile, since a learner may watch a video on desktop but review notes on mobile.
	•	Data privacy: contributor form data (name, role, company if collected) and learner notes should be access-restricted — only admin sees contributor data; only the learner sees their own notes.

7. Delivery Phasing
7.1 MVP (v1)
	•	Landing page with visual phase overview + sign-in.
	•	Phase → Module → Topic navigation (card/tile based).
	•	Embedded tutorial videos per topic + manual progress check-off + notes.
	•	Contributor form (public) → admin review queue → publish workflow.
	•	Admin panel: content database + submission queue.
7.2 V2
	•	Automatic video-progress tracking.
	•	Structured assessments / mini-projects with submission and review, aligned to the Portfolio Projects list (Appendix B).
	•	Case study library (Appendix C) surfaced contextually within relevant modules.
	•	Basic analytics/reporting for the admin on content coverage and learner engagement.
7.3 V3+ (exploratory)
	•	Peer or mentor review of learner-submitted projects.
	•	Community layer connecting contributor PMs with learners.
8. Success Metrics
	•	Activation: % of sign-ups who complete at least one full module.
	•	Depth: average number of phases reached per active learner.
	•	Content growth: number of contributor submissions received and published per month.
	•	Content coverage: % of topics in the taxonomy with at least one published resource.
